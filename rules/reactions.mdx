Reactions are cards installed in Schatten slots that wait for an event instead of a fuse timer. They are face-down until they fire or are revealed.

## How a Reaction is triggered

1. A relevant game event happens, such as the opponent declaring a Release or a wizard taking damage.
2. A Reaction qualifies only if its trigger and filter match and it is not suppressed.
3. If several of your Reactions qualify for the same event, **only the leftmost** fires.
4. If both wizards have qualifying Reactions, the **non-active wizard's** resolves first.
5. The Reaction resolves and moves to the public Record. It does **not** count as a new cast and does not open another declaration window.

Trigger chains have a maximum depth of **3**; events at depth 3 open no additional Reaction triggers.

## Declaration versus damage triggers

The casting pipeline checks opposing declaration triggers before spell effects resolve. A cancellation Reaction such as [Unweave](/cards/unweave) can stop a declared Release, causing the card to go to Record without its effects and clearing the Charge as countered.

An **Unseen** cast skips the *declaration* step. This prevents declaration-triggered reactions such as Unweave, but it does **not** bypass damage and health triggers. Protective effects can still respond to actual damage.

## Prototype examples

- [Ember Ward](/cards/ember-ward) responds to qualifying damage.
- [Stillwater](/cards/stillwater) responds to a sufficiently expensive opposing cast.
- [Unweave](/cards/unweave) counters a Release at declaration.
- [Freeze Frame](/cards/freeze-frame) can suppress an enemy Reaction in Schatten.

## Veil information and bluffing

The opponent sees occupied Schatten slots and public cost/fuse information but not the hidden card identity. Some equipment reveals additional discipline information. Bluffing and playing around the possible response are deliberate strategic choices.
